# Chapter 7: Siege of Shadows

**Lucknow and Jagdishpur**
**August 1857 - April 1858**

As the fire spread, it leaped to the very state where Arjun had been born. **Lucknow** became a furnace.

Arjun and Bishan stood on a hill overlooking the city. Below them, the British were trapped inside the **Residency**, a collection of white buildings now pitted by cannon shells. Leading the charge from the palace was **Begum Hazrat Mahal**.

"She is a woman of the zenana, kaka," Arjun said, watching the Begum’s messengers gallop through the streets. "How does she hold the generals and the taluqdars together?"

Bishan watched a group of farmers—taluqdars who had lost their lands—sharpening their spears. "A mother fights for her son’s heritage. She proclaimed her young son **Birjis Qadr** as the Nawab. In her eyes, and in the eyes of these men, the British are not just rulers; they are thieves who stole their bread. She gives them back their pride."

The siege of the Residency lasted for months, a brutal test of will. But eventually, the British reinforcements under **Sir Colin Campbell** broke through. Lucknow fell in March 1858. The Begum refused to surrender, retreating through the mountains toward Nepal.

"But it wasn't just the Queens and the Nawabs, Arjun," Bishan said as they moved east toward Bihar. "Look at that old man."

In the distance, they saw a massive crowd around a man who looked like he belonged in a temple, not a battlefield. This was **Kunwar Singh** of Jagdishpur. He was eighty years old, his skin like parched earth, but his eyes were bright with a fierce light.

"He is eighty!" Arjun gasped. "Why is he sitting on a horse at this age?"

"Because the Company took his zamindari," Bishan said. "Because he was humiliated by the local British officials. Most men get tired of life at eighty, Arjun. But Kunwar Singh has found a second youth in his anger. He is a tiger who was stepped on once too often."

Arjun heard the legends of the old man—how he outmaneuvered British regiments across the Ganga, how he was shot in the wrist and, rather than let the wound slow him down, cut off his own hand with his sword and offered it to the river.

"He fought until his death in April 1858," Bishan said, his voice lowering in respect. "He died in his own home, undefeated. From the Begums of Lucknow to the old lions of Bihar, the entire land has stood up."

"And yet," Arjun looked at his dusty feet, "one by one, the candles are being blown out."

"The candles are being blown out," Bishan agreed, "but wait until you see the new lamp they are trying to light in their place."

### 🛡️ The History Check (Fact File)

*   **Begum Hazrat Mahal:** She led the revolt in **Lucknow**. She declared her son **Birjis Qadr** as the Nawab and united the taluqdars and sepoys.
*   **Lucknow Siege:** The British Residency was besieged for months. Lucknow was eventually recaptured by **Sir Colin Campbell** in March 1858.
*   **Kunwar Singh:** The 80-year-old zamindar of **Jagdishpur (Bihar)**. He was a brilliant guerrilla leader who harassed British forces across Bihar and Eastern U.P. until his death in 1858.
*   **Other Centers:** The revolt also flared in **Bareilly** (Khan Bahadur Khan) and **Arrah** (Bihar).
